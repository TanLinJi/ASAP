## 1. Purpose / hypothesis

Mirror of `E4.1` but with **PV-RCNN** as the victim detector. This is the first detector-extension gate for the Track A learned-M3 policy. The attacked and purified point clouds are detector-agnostic outputs reused from `E4.1`; the only thing that changes is the detector.

## 2. Dependencies

- `E1.2` (clean baseline).
- `E2.1`, `E2.2`, `E2.3` detector-agnostic attacked sets reused from PointPillars loop.
- Track A policy outputs from `E4.1`: E2.1 b075-ifilter, E2.2/E2.3 b075.
- Revised policy summary: E2.1 b075-ifilter, E2.2 b075, E2.3 skip M3 / no purification.
- Same ASAP implementation as `E4.1`.

## 3. Setup

Same structure as `E4.1` §3, with:

- Detector config: `tools/cfgs/kitti_models/pv_rcnn.yaml`.
- Detector checkpoint: `checkpoints/kitti/pv_rcnn_8369.pth`.
- Batch size: 4 total under two-GPU distributed eval (`NUM_GPUS=2`, batch size 4).

## 4. Procedure

Same procedure as `E4.1`, swapping the detector / config / checkpoint. The run evaluated:

1. No-defense PV-RCNN on `outputs/attacks/kitti/E2.{1,2,3}_*/velodyne_attacked`.
2. Track A policy PV-RCNN on:
   - `outputs/asap_vpsde_score_net_anchor_b075_ifilter/kitti/E2.1_injection/velodyne_purified`
   - `outputs/asap_vpsde_score_net_anchor_b075/kitti/E2.2_perturbation/velodyne_purified`
   - `outputs/asap_vpsde_score_net_anchor_b075/kitti/E2.3_dropping/velodyne_purified`
3. Revised policy aggregation with E2.3 Dropping set to no-defense / skip M3.
4. SOR/ROR PV-RCNN baselines on the same defended point clouds used in `E4.1`.

Executed in tmux with two T4 GPUs:

```bash
tmux new-session -d -s asap_e42_pvrcnn_tracka_20260530_105446 \

```

## 7. Analysis

The detector-extension result is mixed:

- **Injection passes the cross-detector gate.** E2.1 improves from 63.16 to 72.59 Car AP and from 48.22 to 57.73 mAP. This supports the Injection radius-filter branch as a detector-agnostic component, not just a PointPillars artifact.
- **Perturbation improves but remains weak in absolute terms.** E2.2 improves from 3.50 to 10.96 Car AP. The perturbation attack is far stronger for PV-RCNN than for PointPillars, so the policy recovers signal but does not make this row paper-ready.
- **Track B pfilter did not fix the PV-RCNN Perturbation weakness.** A targeted `ASAP-pfilter` diagnostic on E2.2 with `score_filter_attack_kinds=perturbation`, `radius=0.4`, and `min_neighbors=5` reached **10.53** Car AP / **4.99** mAP, below Track A's **10.96** / **5.98**. It edited **21.93%** of points and then removed another **5.32%**, suggesting over-removal of sparse useful evidence. See `E5.6`.
- **Lower anchor blend is also negative.** A non-deletion Track B variant with `score_anchor_blend=0.50` reached **8.31** Car AP / **5.03** mAP, below Track A's **10.96** / **5.98**. The local anchor appears to stabilize the current score-net rather than over-constrain it.
- **A modest larger `t_star` is positive.** Keeping `score_anchor_blend=0.75`, disabling support filtering, and increasing `t_star` from `0.05` to `0.08` reaches **11.23** Car AP / **6.26** mAP. This is a small but consistent improvement over Track A, and the same purified scans also improve PointPillars E2.2 to **61.26** / **34.76**.
- **A larger `t_star=0.10` is not promoted.** It reaches **11.36** Car AP on PV-RCNN, but mAP falls to **6.16** and PointPillars Car AP falls to **61.06**. The main claim needs cross-class and cross-detector consistency, so `tstar008` remains the paper-facing row.
- **Dropping fails under learned M3 but is fixed by the skip rule.** E2.3 Track A policy drops from 78.61 to 76.15 Car AP and from 57.12 to 42.45 mAP. The revised policy therefore skips M3 for `attack_kind == "dropping"`, restoring the no-defense attacked result by construction: 78.61 Car AP and 57.12 mAP.
- **ROR is still marginally best on Injection.** PV-RCNN ROR reaches 72.77 Car AP / 57.77 mAP, slightly above revised ASAP's 72.59 / 57.73. The margin is small, but the paper should not claim per-row dominance on Injection.

Conclusion: E4.2 validates the revised policy as a cross-attack conservative candidate, not a per-row winner. ASAP is strongest on Perturbation and safest on Dropping; ROR remains a very strong classical baseline for Injection. For E2.2 Perturbation, `t_star=0.08` is the current KITTI candidate, while the absolute PV-RCNN recovery remains limited.

## 8. Issues encountered & resolutions

- Distributed PV-RCNN eval sometimes emitted a trailing zero-byte `log_eval_*.txt`. Resolution: `scripts/parse_eval_summary.py` now ignores empty eval logs and selects the latest non-empty log.
- All long evaluations were run in `tmux` with `NUM_GPUS=2`. `eval_attacked_kitti.sh` restored `data/kitti/training/velodyne` after every cell.

## 9. Next actions

- Treat the revised policy as the current KITTI two-detector candidate, but phrase the claim as robustness across attack families rather than per-row superiority.
- If the paper needs an Injection win against ROR, test a meta-policy that uses ROR-like filtering for Injection and learned M3 for Perturbation, while keeping Dropping skip.
- For the PV-RCNN Perturbation gap, the direct pfilter transfer and lower `score_anchor_blend` are both negative, while `t_star=0.08` is positive. Use `tstar008` for the paper-facing KITTI Perturbation rows, but still discuss the low absolute PV-RCNN recovery as a limitation.
- Do not continue the `t_star` micro-sweep without a new objective; `t_star=0.10` improves only PV-RCNN Car AP and weakens the consistency story.
- Otherwise, move to the next detector/dataset and keep ROR as a strong classical baseline in the KITTI table.
